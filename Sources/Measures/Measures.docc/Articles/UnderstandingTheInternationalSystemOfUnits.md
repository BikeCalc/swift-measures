# Understanding the International System of Units

Learn how base units combine to form derived units in the International System of Units.

## Overview

The International System of Units, abbreviated SI, is the internationally agreed system of measurement. It specifies
seven base units, one for each base quantity. Derived units are formed by multiplying, dividing, or raising base units
and other derived units to powers.

A physical quantity has a dimension and is expressed using a unit. For example, a distance is a quantity with the
dimension of length and can be expressed in meters.

A derived unit does not need a special name. Force uses the specially named unit newton (`N`), while speed uses the
compound unit meter per second (`m/s`). Both are SI derived units.

A coherent SI unit is related to the SI base units without an additional numerical conversion factor. A coherent
derived unit is not itself an SI base unit. For example, the pascal is the coherent derived unit of pressure and is
equal to one newton per square meter.

## Base Units

| Dimension | Base unit | Symbol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amount of substance | mole | `mol` |
| Electric current | ampere | `A` |
| Length | meter | `m` |
| Luminous intensity | candela | `cd` |
| Mass | kilogram | `kg` |
| Thermodynamic temperature | kelvin | `K` |
| Time | second | `s` |

## Derived Units

| Dimension | Composition | Base unit | Symbol |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Absorbed dose | energy ÷ mass | gray | `Gy` |
| Acceleration | speed ÷ time | meter per square second | `m/s²` |
| Area | length × length | square meter | `m²` |
| Catalytic activity | amount of substance ÷ time | katal | `kat` |
| Electric charge | electric current × time | coulomb | `C` |
| Electric conductance | electric current ÷ electric potential | siemens | `S` |
| Electric potential | power ÷ electric current | volt | `V` |
| Electrical capacitance | electric charge ÷ electric potential | farad | `F` |
| Electrical inductance | magnetic flux ÷ electric current | henry | `H` |
| Electrical resistance | electric potential ÷ electric current | ohm | `Ω` |
| Energy | force × length | joule | `J` |
| Equivalent dose | absorbed dose × weighting factor | sievert | `Sv` |
| Force | mass × acceleration | newton | `N` |
| Frequency | one ÷ time | hertz | `Hz` |
| Illuminance | luminous flux ÷ area | lux | `lx` |
| Luminous flux | luminous intensity × solid angle | lumen | `lm` |
| Magnetic flux | electric potential × time | weber | `Wb` |
| Magnetic flux density | magnetic flux ÷ area | tesla | `T` |
| Mass concentration | mass ÷ volume | kilogram per cubic meter | `kg/m³` |
| Molar mass | mass ÷ amount of substance | kilogram per mole | `kg/mol` |
| Power | energy ÷ time | watt | `W` |
| Pressure | force ÷ area | pascal | `Pa` |
| Radioactivity | decays ÷ time | becquerel | `Bq` |
| Speed | length ÷ time | meter per second | `m/s` |
| Surface density | mass ÷ area | kilogram per square meter | `kg/m²` |
| Volume | area × length | cubic meter | `m³` |

Different quantities can share the same dimension and coherent unit expression. For example, frequency and
radioactivity both have the dimension of reciprocal time, while absorbed dose and equivalent dose both use joules per
kilogram. Their distinct unit names preserve the meanings of the quantities they represent.
